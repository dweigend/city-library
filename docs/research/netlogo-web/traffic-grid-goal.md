# Traffic Grid Goal

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 129 words

The scientific question is whether explicit home-work destinations change congestion relative to undirected circulating traffic. In NetLogo's Traffic Grid Goal model, cars alternate between assigned home and work patches, selecting movement toward their destination while obeying car-following and traffic-light rules. The selected literature connects this mechanism to goal-directed commuting and signal control: the source textbook supplies this concrete destination-based traffic variant [1]; adaptive signal coordination provides a related control mechanism [2]; empirical microscopic traffic states constrain interpretation of congestion patterns [3]. A regular grid and repeated fixed destinations omit heterogeneous schedules and adaptive routing; destination completion is different from simply counting moving cars. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
