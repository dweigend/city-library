# Bidding Market

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 124 words

The scientific question is whether local price adjustment can coordinate buyers and sellers without an auctioneer. In NetLogo's Bidding Market model, buyers rotate between sellers, transact when prices meet their thresholds and adapt expectations while sellers adjust asking prices. The selected literature connects this mechanism to decentralized exchange and price adaptation: experimental exchange provides a benchmark for price convergence [1]; market efficiency need not require sophisticated individual calculation [2]; competitive settings depend on spatial and institutional assumptions [3]. Finite inventories, one-unit purchases and heuristic adjustments differ from a continuous double auction and do not guarantee a competitive equilibrium. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
