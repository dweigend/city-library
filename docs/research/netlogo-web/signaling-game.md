# Signaling Game

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 123 words

The scientific question is whether successful reinforcement can establish shared meanings without preassigned signal semantics. In NetLogo's Signaling Game model, senders select signals for world states, receivers choose responses, and rewarded pairings become more likely in subsequent interactions. The selected literature connects this mechanism to coordination through learned signaling conventions: conventions solve recurrent coordination problems without explicit agreement [1]; evolution and learning explain emergence and failure of signaling systems [2]; equilibrium provides a benchmark distinct from the simulated learning process [3]. Aligned payoffs, finite vocabularies and simple learning restrict claims about natural language, deception and unequal interests. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
