# Virus on a Network

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 116 words

The scientific question is whether contact structure and periodic detection change infection persistence. In NetLogo's Virus on a Network model, susceptible nodes can become infected through neighbors, while checks may remove infection and sometimes produce permanent resistance. The selected literature connects this mechanism to network-mediated contagion and control: graph epidemiology supplies a computer-virus transmission framework [1]; degree heterogeneity can change epidemic spreading behavior [2]; classical epidemic compartments clarify recovery and immunity assumptions [3]. The susceptible-infected-resistant rules and scheduled checking differ from many continuous-time epidemic equations; computer-network interpretation is an analogy. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
