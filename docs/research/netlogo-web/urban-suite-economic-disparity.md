# Urban Suite - Economic Disparity

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 139 words

The scientific question is whether income-dependent location preferences reinforce residential and service inequality. In NetLogo's Urban Suite - Economic Disparity model, rich and poor entrants sample sites with different utility priorities, influence local price and quality, and attract jobs toward higher-value areas. The selected literature connects this mechanism to economic sorting and reinforcing land-use change: the acknowledged SOME ancestry confronts empirical requirements for residential development [1]; economic segregation models connect relocation choices with spatial inequality [2]; social context cautions against explaining observed separation from abstract preferences alone [3]. The source itself questions the assumption that jobs seek expensive land; binary income groups and directional neighborhood effects are propositions rather than established causal laws. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
