# Language Change

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 119 words

The scientific question is whether speaker learning and social-network structure jointly change linguistic variants. In NetLogo's Language Change model, connected speakers produce utterances from competing grammars and alter their grammar use after hearing neighboring speakers. The selected literature connects this mechanism to network-mediated linguistic competition: utterance selection formalizes stochastic language-change mechanisms [1]; language competition offers a contrasting aggregate dynamics model [2]; degree-biased network growth contextualizes the models heterogeneous social connections [3]. Binary variants and simplified learning omit linguistic content and institutional prestige; language death is a related aggregate comparator, not this exact mechanism. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
