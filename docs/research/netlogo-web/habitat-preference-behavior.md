# Habitat Preference Behavior

Status: complete. Scientific research only.

Can different rates of random movement produce an apparent habitat preference? NetLogo Habitat Preference Behavior recreates a two-chamber pillbug experiment with moist or dry conditions. Agents move randomly, but in their preferred environment they move only half the time. Original humidity-response experiments [1], mechanistic isopod research [2], and terrestrial-isopod biology [3] provide direct scientific context. The model therefore illustrates retention through changed activity rather than requiring agents to navigate toward a known destination. Its binary conditions and fixed activity probability simplify sensory responses, water balance, and species differences. Unequal chamber occupancy is an outcome of these rules, not evidence of conscious choice, a complete behavioral mechanism, or validation against measured pillbug movement.
