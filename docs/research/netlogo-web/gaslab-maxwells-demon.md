# GasLab Maxwells Demon

Research status: complete. Literature research only; implementation acceptance is separate.

NetLogo GasLab Maxwells Demon investigates what selective access to molecular velocities implies for entropy accounting. Elastic gas particles move and collide in two chambers while an idealized valve transfers fast particles one way and slow particles the other. Connects information and entropy through an idealized measurement-controlled engine [1]. Identifies thermodynamic consequences of logically irreversible information processing [2]. Explains computation and memory within the resolution of demon-like thermodynamic puzzles [3]. The NetLogo valve omits a physical sensor, controller memory and their energetic costs; separating temperatures therefore cannot establish a working violation of thermodynamics. The simulation illustrates why observing a sorting mechanism requires a larger accounting boundary around the controller. Reproducing these patterns establishes rule fidelity, not empirical validation.
