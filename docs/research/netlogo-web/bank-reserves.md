# Bank Reserves

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 128 words

The scientific question is whether a stipulated reserve ratio controls lending and recorded money creation. In NetLogo's Bank Reserves model, random payments change wallets, after which agents deposit savings or request loans from one aggregate bank subject to available reserves. The selected literature connects this mechanism to lending constraints and money-accounting rules: bank-accounting evidence tests how lending creates deposits [1]; contrasting banking theories expose dependence on institutional assumptions [2]; modern monetary analysis rejects a literal mechanical money-multiplier interpretation [3]. The model imposes a fractional-reserve mechanism rather than demonstrating that modern banks mechanically multiply deposits; capital, credit demand and monetary policy are abstracted. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
