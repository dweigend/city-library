# Sex Ratio Equilibrium

Status: complete. Scientific research only.

Why can frequency-dependent selection favor a balanced sex ratio? NetLogo Sex Ratio Equilibrium follows inherited reproductive tendencies under biparental reproduction and Mendelian assumptions. Fisher's argument [1] supplies the central intuition: the rarer sex can have a reproductive advantage. Hamilton's analysis [2] and sex-allocation theory [3] identify conditions under which ratios can differ. The served model investigates an adult demographic ratio with particular inheritance and reproductive rules; adult ratios must not automatically be equated with parental investment at conception. Equilibrium-like output illustrates those assumptions, not a biological law requiring every population to contain equal numbers of males and females. Differential survival, mating structure, and unequal reproductive costs can change the interpretation.
