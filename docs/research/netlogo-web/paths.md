# Paths

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 123 words

The scientific question is whether reinforcement by repeated walkers can create a shared path network. In NetLogo's Paths model, travelers head toward random destinations while favoring established routes and increasing the popularity of patches they traverse. The selected literature connects this mechanism to environment-mediated trail formation: active-walker feedback connects pedestrian motion with emerging trails [1]; trail models distinguish destination-seeking walkers from other moving agents [2]; increasing returns explain why early route use can influence later choices [3]. Popularity thresholds and movement heuristics omit construction costs, legal access and measured pedestrian preferences; routes need not be globally optimal. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
