# Wolf Sheep Predation

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 131 words

The scientific question is whether local consumption, reproduction and resource renewal permit predator-prey coexistence. In NetLogo's Wolf Sheep Predation model, mobile wolves eat sheep and expend energy; the optional grass variant gives sheep a renewable food supply and explicit energy costs. The selected literature connects this mechanism to population feedback and ecological model construction: coupled population changes provide the classical predator-prey comparison [1]; embodied predator-prey modeling connects individual rules to aggregate dynamics [2]; experimental population competition highlights the need for biological evidence [3]. Random movement, fixed reproduction probabilities and simplified energy budgets omit species-specific behavior; aggregate differential equations are theoretical comparators rather than identical implementations. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
