# Heroes and Cowards

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 123 words

The scientific question is whether simple friend-enemy positioning rules produce organized collective motion. In NetLogo's Heroes and Cowards model, each agent chooses a friend and enemy and moves either between them or behind its friend relative to the enemy. The selected literature connects this mechanism to collective spatial organization: the source textbook presents this specific friend-enemy thought experiment [1]; distributed movement rules demonstrate formation without centralized control [2]; collective-motion models relate local interactions to spatial organization [3]. Fixed relationships and geometric movement rules omit motives, perception errors and human social interpretation; bravery is an assigned rule label. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
