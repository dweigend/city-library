# Braess Paradox

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 130 words

The scientific question is whether adding a fast route can worsen travel times under selfish route choice. In NetLogo's Braess Paradox model, commuters choose between network routes whose costs depend on congestion, allowing comparison before and after a shortcut is opened. The selected literature connects this mechanism to selfish routing and collective inefficiency: the source-cited paradox demonstrates harmful added capacity [1]; user-equilibrium conditions distinguish individual route optimality from system optimality [2]; inefficiency bounds formalize the consequences of selfish routing [3]. The paradox depends on the cost functions and demand; additional capacity does not universally worsen networks and adaptive runs need not instantly reach equilibrium. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
