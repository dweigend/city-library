# Slime

Status: complete. Scientific research only.

Can chemical attraction produce aggregation without a pacemaker cell? NetLogo Slime lets mobile cells respond to a chemical field that they also help create. Local reinforcement can turn an initially dispersed population into clusters. Keller and Segel's instability framework [1] is explicitly named in the Info; experimental chemotaxis [2] and Dictyostelium biology [3] contextualize the mechanism. The model reduces development to movement, signaling, diffusion, and signal loss, without reconstructing complete multicellular differentiation or a measured biochemical network. Its clusters illustrate a possible decentralized aggregation mechanism. They do not demonstrate that all slime molds share this life cycle, or that a successful run quantitatively matches an organism under laboratory conditions.
