# Current in a Semiconductor

Research status: complete. Literature research only; implementation acceptance is separate.

NetLogo Current in a Semiconductor investigates how carrier generation, drift and recombination combine to produce conduction. Thermally generated electron-hole pairs and doped carriers respond to applied voltage, scatter probabilistically, and may annihilate when electrons encounter holes. Provides the classical drift-and-scattering foundation explicitly adapted by the source [1]. Provides the semiconductor-junction theory context beyond a free-carrier cartoon [2]. Analyzes carrier generation and recombination, directly relevant to the model population balance [3]. The source neglects carrier interactions apart from recombination and does not solve a quantum band structure or self-consistent electrostatic field; apparent temperature dependence is therefore schematic. The simulation illustrates the separate roles of carrier abundance, applied driving and interruption of directed motion. Reproducing these patterns establishes rule fidelity, not empirical validation.
