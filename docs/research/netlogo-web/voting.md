# Voting

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 119 words

The scientific question is whether local majority decisions create persistent spatial agreement or oscillation. In NetLogo's Voting model, binary patches synchronously consult eight neighbors, with optional switches that reverse ties or narrow majorities. The selected literature connects this mechanism to local interaction and opinion dynamics: interacting particle systems contextualize local state dependence [1]; majority-based opinion aggregation exposes sensitivity to decision rules [2]; opinion-model comparisons clarify why different update mechanisms matter [3]. These deterministic neighborhood rules omit strategic voting, institutions and measured human preferences; the title does not make this a calibrated election model. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
