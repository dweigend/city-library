# Urban Suite - Tijuana Bordertowns

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 134 words

The scientific question is whether migration, income and infrastructure access can jointly shape informal settlement. In NetLogo's Urban Suite - Tijuana Bordertowns model, migrants enter near compatriots, earn and spend money, seek affordable serviced locations and form settlements whose density can trigger infrastructure development. The selected literature connects this mechanism to migration-linked settlement and urban-service inequalities: scholarly border-city studies supply context for Tijuanas distinctive urban conditions [1]; empirical requirements constrain bottom-up residential development modeling [2]; observed urban-expansion research illustrates the need for explicit spatial evidence [3]. The source is a pedagogical design experiment with stylized economic and migration rules; no contemporary Tijuana forecast or calibration is established here. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
