# Wealth Distribution

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 126 words

The scientific question is whether heterogeneous resource gathering can generate unequal wealth without explicit inheritance. In NetLogo's Wealth Distribution model, agents seek grain, consume it at individual metabolic rates and are replaced after starvation or age-related death. The selected literature connects this mechanism to resource-mediated inequality and distributional inference: Sugarscape supplies the explicitly acknowledged resource-gathering ancestry [1]; wealth-distribution mechanisms offer a contrasting statistical-physics account [2]; tail-fitting methods constrain claims about emergent Pareto behavior [3]. Grain wealth, replacement endowments and spatial access abstract away wages, financial institutions and inherited capital; apparent distributional similarity does not identify the real cause of inequality. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
