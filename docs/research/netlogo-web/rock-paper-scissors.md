# Rock Paper Scissors

Status: complete. Scientific research only.

How does movement influence coexistence among cyclic competitors? NetLogo Rock Paper Scissors represents three local populations: red defeats green, green defeats blue, and blue defeats red. Reproduction, competition, and exchange of positions generate spatial patterns. The Info connects its event-based formulation to mobility studies [1,2], while bacterial competition experiments [3] provide an empirical comparison. The agents are abstract competitors rather than a calibrated microbial species assemblage; interaction symmetry and movement rates are declared assumptions. Spirals illustrate how local cyclic dominance can organize space and how mobility can change persistence. They do not prove that diversity is always stable, that more movement is always beneficial, or that identical rates describe real ecological interactions.
