# Cash Flow

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 122 words

The scientific question is whether reserve constraints and local payments jointly alter money holdings and inequality. In NetLogo's Cash Flow model, mobile agents exchange money, settle wallet balances through savings and loans, and interact with a single aggregate bank. The selected literature connects this mechanism to distributional accounting and banking assumptions: empirical bank-accounting analysis challenges simple intermediation interpretations [1]; comparison of banking theories tests alternative credit-creation accounts [2]; central-bank research directly qualifies mechanical deposit-multiplier reasoning [3]. A reserve-driven money multiplier is a pedagogical assumption; modern credit creation, interest and lending constraints require a richer institutional account. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
