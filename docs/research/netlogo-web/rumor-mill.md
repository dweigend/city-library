# Rumor Mill

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 125 words

The scientific question is whether spatial proximity changes the speed and redundancy of information spread. In NetLogo's Rumor Mill model, informed patches repeatedly tell a random neighbor, using either four-neighbor or eight-neighbor contact and selectable initial seeding. The selected literature connects this mechanism to local information transmission: stochastic rumor theory provides a comparator with explicit transmission states [1]; network structure influences rumor spreading beyond homogeneous mixing [2]; behavioral-spread experiments show why repeated exposure need not act like simple information transfer [3]. Information is retained and repeatedly transmitted without credibility assessment; rumor models with stifling therefore have different stopping rules. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
