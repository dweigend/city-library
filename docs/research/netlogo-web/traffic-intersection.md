# Traffic Intersection

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 135 words

The scientific question is whether signal schedules can reduce waiting while avoiding conflicts between crossing flows. In NetLogo's Traffic Intersection model, cars enter from two directions, accelerate and brake subject to limits, stop for signals and disappear after a modeled collision. The selected literature connects this mechanism to intersection control and constrained vehicle response: traffic performance depends on the distinction between individual and system objectives [1]; adaptive signals offer an alternative to fixed timing [2]; microscopic acceleration and braking require comparison with real driving behavior [3]. Arrival rules, braking limits and one intersection exclude route diversion and wider-network spillback; minimizing a waiting counter alone is not a safety evaluation. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
