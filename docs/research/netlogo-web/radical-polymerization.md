# Radical Polymerization

Research status: complete. Literature research only; implementation acceptance is separate.

NetLogo Radical Polymerization investigates how radical availability and monomer encounters shape polymerization kinetics. Mobile monomers and active radical agents interact locally; propagation consumes monomers while radical encounters terminate activity, making the remaining population change over time. Provides the chemical mechanism of free-radical initiation, growth and termination [1]. Provides the finite-population reaction-simulation context [2]. Provides a molecular-size perspective that helps identify what a reduced population-level polymerization representation cannot establish [3]. The original educational analogy simplifies reaction chemistry and spatial encounters; agreement with its declining monomer curve does not validate industrial kinetics or recover a chemically realistic chain-length distribution. The simulation illustrates how microscopic encounter rules produce nonlinear population-level consumption and termination. Reproducing these patterns establishes rule fidelity, not empirical validation.
