# Traffic Grid

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 125 words

The scientific question is whether signal timing and vehicle density jointly determine urban-grid congestion. In NetLogo's Traffic Grid model, cars accelerate and follow other vehicles while stopping at red lights, whose phases can be controlled locally or automatically. The selected literature connects this mechanism to network congestion and signal coordination: grid interactions can generate a transition to collective blockage [1]; self-organizing traffic lights provide a contrasting control strategy [2]; microscopic traffic-state comparisons caution against uncalibrated travel predictions [3]. Regular roads, simplified car following and limited route choice omit heterogeneous trips and intersections; waiting counts and throughput measure different objectives. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
