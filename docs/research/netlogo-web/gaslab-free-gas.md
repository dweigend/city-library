# GasLab Free Gas

Research status: complete. Literature research only; implementation acceptance is separate.

NetLogo GasLab Free Gas investigates how freely moving elastic particles redistribute molecular speeds. Particles travel along straight segments and exchange momentum and kinetic energy in local pair collisions, with coloring used to display their instantaneous speeds. Develops the kinetic collision and speed-distribution foundations [1]. Provides an early computational hard-sphere reference and exposes the distinction between detailed molecular dynamics and simplified collision rules [2]. Supplies the statistical-equilibrium perspective; the cited record is a later reprint of the historical work [3]. The world and pair-selection rules are idealized, internal molecular energy and long-range interactions are absent, and displayed particle count is far below a macroscopic gas. The simulation illustrates how conserved collision events can coexist with changing individual speeds. Reproducing these patterns establishes rule fidelity, not empirical validation.
