# Enzyme Kinetics

Research status: complete. Literature research only; implementation acceptance is separate.

NetLogo Enzyme Kinetics investigates why catalytic reaction rates saturate and change under inhibition. Enzymes and substrates associate into complexes, complexes dissociate or release product, and inhibitor molecules can compete for available enzymes. Revisits and translates the original 1913 experiments, including the distinction between fitted specificity and the modern Michaelis constant [1]. Provides the kinetic treatment of enzyme-substrate intermediates [2]. Provides a stochastic-reaction reference for fluctuations and reaction-event interpretation [3]. The NetLogo interaction probabilities and spatial encounters are pedagogical; quasi-steady-state and well-mixed assumptions must be checked before fitting its outputs to a Michaelis-Menten law. The simulation illustrates finite enzyme capacity, substrate dependence and competitive occupation without establishing measured biochemical parameters. Reproducing these patterns establishes rule fidelity, not empirical validation.
