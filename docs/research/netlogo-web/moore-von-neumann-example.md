# Moore & Von Neumann Example

Status: complete. Scientific research checked 2026-11-05.

How does the definition of a neighborhood change which cells can interact? NetLogo Moore and Von Neumann Example constructs square and diamond neighborhoods of selectable radius, extending the built-in radius-one primitives. Toffoli and Margolus [1] provide cellular-automaton implementation foundations, Wolfram [2] analyzes behavior generated by local interactions, and Fisch and colleagues [3] examine threshold and range in spatial automata. The square corresponds to coordinate-wise distance bounds, while the diamond restricts the sum of coordinate differences. The example supports wrapping and nonwrapping worlds, so boundary handling affects actual membership. It demonstrates a geometric contract rather than an autonomous scientific process. Different neighborhood cardinalities or inclusion of the center can change downstream dynamics even if update rules look identical. The simulation illustrates why topology, radius, and edge conventions must be explicit before comparing spatial models.
