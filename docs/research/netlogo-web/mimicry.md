# Mimicry

Status: complete. Scientific research only.

How can predator learning favor resemblance between defended and undefended prey? NetLogo Mimicry represents birds that remember unpleasant prey colors and butterflies that reproduce with occasional color changes. Avoidance can favor similar appearances without deliberate imitation. Bates's original work [1] and experiments on monarch–viceroy mimicry [2] provide historical scientific context. Later evidence that viceroys are themselves unpalatable [3] challenges the particular example, as the Info acknowledges. The model assumes a simplified memory capacity, color space, and reproductive process. It illustrates selection mediated by learning, not a verified reconstruction of monarch–viceroy evolution. Neither matching colors nor predator avoidance establishes that the modeled species relationship is genuinely Batesian.
