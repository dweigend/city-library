# Party

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 120 words

The scientific question is whether individual comfort thresholds lead groups to sort even without a central organizer. In NetLogo's Party model, partygoers leave groups whose opposite-type share exceeds their tolerance and search for another group. The selected literature connects this mechanism to threshold-based group sorting: local composition preferences can produce aggregate separation [1]; segregation dynamics depend on relocation incentives and admissible alternatives [2]; real social context limits interpretation of stylized sorting patterns [3]. The source uses binary gender categories and uniform tolerance; these are historical modeling conventions rather than supported descriptions of actual parties. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
