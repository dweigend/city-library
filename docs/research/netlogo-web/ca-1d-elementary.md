# CA 1D Elementary

Status: complete. Scientific research checked 2026-11-05.

How many different behaviors can arise from the same tiny neighborhood? NetLogo CA 1D Elementary explores the 256 binary rules mapping a cell and its immediate left and right neighbors to one next-state bit. Each new row records the next generation, so the display combines space and time rather than representing a two-dimensional spatial population. Wolfram [1] supplies the foundational statistical study, Li and colleagues [2] compare transitions across rule space, and Chaté and Manneville [3] analyze critical behavior in cellular automata. Single-cell and random initializations expose sensitivity to starting conditions. Rule numbering, boundary wrapping, and continuation at the bottom are part of the baseline. Finite patterns may resemble chaos or nested structure, but visual classification alone does not prove randomness, universality, or correspondence with a measured natural process.
