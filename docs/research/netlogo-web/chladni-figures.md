# Chladni Figures

Research status: complete. Literature research only; implementation acceptance is separate.

NetLogo Chladni Figures investigates why grains can accumulate near nodes of an imposed vibration pattern. Each grain moves in a random direction with speed determined by a sum of sinusoidal distance-based contributions, slowing where that imposed amplitude approaches zero. Investigates acoustic figures and particle arrangements on vibrating surfaces [1]. Analyzes normal vibrating modes of square plates [2]. Provides classical standing-wave theory; the DOI identifies a modern reissue [3]. The source computes a prescribed pattern rather than solving the elasticity of a plate or detailed grain contact mechanics; its distance-based field need not be an exact plate eigenmode. The simulation illustrates node-seeking accumulation while distinguishing a visual mechanism from a calibrated vibration experiment. Reproducing these patterns establishes rule fidelity, not empirical validation.

The beautiful series of forms assumed by sand, filings, or other grains, when lying upon vibrating plates, discovered and developed by Chladni, are so striking as to be recalled to the minds of those who have seen them by the slightest reference. Chladni observed that shavings from the hairs of the exciting violin bow did not proceed to the nodal lines, but were gathered together on those parts of the plate the most violently agitated, i. e. at the centres of oscillation. Thus when a square plate of glass held horizontally was nipped above and below at the centre, and made to vibrate by the application of a violin bow to the middle of one edge, so as to produce the lowest possible sound, sand sprinkled on the plate assumed the form of a diagonal cross; but the light shavings were gathered together at those parts towards the middle of the four portions where the vibrations were most powerful and the excursions of the plate greatest.
