# Prisoner's Dilemma Two Person Iterated

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 125 words

The scientific question is whether repeated encounters allow contingent strategies to sustain cooperation. In NetLogo's Prisoner's Dilemma Two Person Iterated model, two players repeatedly choose cooperation or defection, score outcomes and respond using rules based on previous interaction. The selected literature connects this mechanism to reciprocity and repeated strategic interaction: computer tournaments compare contingent prisoner-dilemma strategies [1]; reciprocity supplies a mechanism for the persistence of cooperation [2]; other evolutionary mechanisms qualify explanations based solely on direct reciprocity [3]. Strategy choices, noise and horizon assumptions strongly affect outcomes; a winning simulated strategy is not a universal prescription for social cooperation. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
