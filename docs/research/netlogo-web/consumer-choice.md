# Consumer Choice

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 122 words

The scientific question is whether social information and conformity can compete with product quality and habit. In NetLogo's Consumer Choice model, networked consumers evaluate two products using quality experience, friends information, normative utility and repeated choice. The selected literature connects this mechanism to socially embedded consumption: habit and changing preferences offer a consumer-learning comparison [1]; informative and normative influence distinguish reasons for conformity [2]; norm-dependent behavior can create collective tipping and policy sensitivity [3]. Fixed behavioral rules and a two-product market omit income constraints and strategic pricing; a simulated market share is not a demand estimate. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
