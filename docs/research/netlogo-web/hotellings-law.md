# Hotelling's Law

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 129 words

The scientific question is whether location and price competition produce clustering or differentiation. In NetLogo's Hotelling's Law model, stores consider moves and price changes while consumers choose the minimum combined travel and purchase cost on a line or plane. The selected literature connects this mechanism to spatial competition and equilibrium qualifications: the explicitly acknowledged original model links location to competition [1]; the classical correction identifies limits to Hotellings equilibrium claims [2]; equilibrium is a strategic benchmark rather than guaranteed heuristic convergence [3]. Discrete local search, planar extension and competitor ignorance differ from analytical equilibrium assumptions; transport costs can alter the original clustering result. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
