# Minority Game

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 121 words

The scientific question is whether adaptive prediction can coordinate competition for a scarce winning position. In NetLogo's Minority Game model, agents choose binary sides using their best-scoring strategy tables, rewarding the minority and updating hypothetical strategy scores. The selected literature connects this mechanism to adaptive crowding and coordination: the acknowledged minority-game mechanism rewards anti-crowding choices [1]; heterogeneous strategy interactions explain collective fluctuations [2]; evolving prediction strategies provide a critical El Farol comparison [3]. Public binary histories and a fixed strategy pool omit prices, budgets and real market institutions; success is defined solely by minority membership. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
