# Three Doors

Status: complete. Scientific research checked 2026-11-05.

When does changing a choice improve its probability of success? NetLogo Three Doors repeatedly simulates the Monty Hall problem: a prize and initial choice are randomized, the knowledgeable host leaves an alternative closed door, and agents switch according to individual propensities. Morgan and colleagues [1] analyze the player's dilemma and the importance of the host protocol. Jaynes [2] provides conditional-probability reasoning, while Durrett [3] supplies a formal sample-space framework. The agent race turns repeated wins into visible evidence for comparing switching policies. The result depends on a host who avoids revealing the prize and always offers the specified alternative; different disclosure rules create different conditional problems. Finite races fluctuate, and mixed switching propensities are strategies rather than psychological estimates. The simulation illustrates conditioning under an explicit information-generating process.
