# El Farol

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 124 words

The scientific question is whether independent boundedly rational forecasts can coordinate attendance near a capacity threshold. In NetLogo's El Farol model, agents score attendance predictors against recent history and attend when their selected forecast predicts an uncrowded bar. The selected literature connects this mechanism to inductive reasoning and adaptive crowding: the original bar problem formalizes inductive expectation formation [1]; evolutionary forecasting critically extends the original bounded-rationality model [2]; minority rewards isolate a related anti-crowding coordination mechanism [3]. Predictor design, memory and threshold choices shape results; convergence of mean attendance does not imply accurate shared beliefs or full rationality. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
