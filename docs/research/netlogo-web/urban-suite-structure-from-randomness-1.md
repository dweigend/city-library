# Urban Suite - Structure from Randomness 1

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 131 words

The scientific question is whether local averaging can produce spatial structure from unstructured initial variation. In NetLogo's Urban Suite - Structure from Randomness 1 model, binary random activity values are repeatedly averaged across each cell and its four neighbors, optionally with continuing random input. The selected literature connects this mechanism to local spatial dependence and emergent pattern: the acknowledged source develops this specific structure-from-randomness example [1]; spatial simulation provides an early urban-growth comparison [2]; urban cellular models connect local interactions to aggregate land-use form [3]. Activity is an abstract scalar without a calibrated urban interpretation; smoothing-generated patterns do not by themselves demonstrate a city-growth mechanism. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
