# Robotic Factory

Status: complete. Scientific research checked 2026-11-05.

Can decentralized transport sustain production when robots face energy constraints and processing bottlenecks? NetLogo Robotic Factory moves materials through cutters, stitchers, finishers, storage, and loading facilities using locally controlled robots. Different garment types and processing times create unequal demands; robots carrying products spend energy and must return to recharge. Chi and colleagues [1] analyze misconceptions about emergent causation, Wilensky and Resnick [2] distinguish agent-level actions from system-level patterns, and Hölldobler and Wilson [3] provide the social-insect analogy cited by the original model. The simulation illustrates how transport, charging, and queues jointly affect throughput. Its fixed factory layout, simplified jobs, and specified energy rules are assumptions rather than measured industrial parameters. Productive-looking self-organization does not demonstrate optimal scheduling, real factory resilience, or superiority over a well-designed centralized controller.
