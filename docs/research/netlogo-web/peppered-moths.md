# Peppered Moths

Status: complete. Scientific research only.

How can environmental change reverse selection on a visible trait? NetLogo Peppered Moths gives moth colors different predation risks against changing backgrounds and follows their frequencies across reproduction. Kettlewell's experiments [1] establish the historical case; later predation work [2] and molecular research [3] strengthen and refine its interpretation. The served model represents predation invisibly and simplifies inheritance, pollution, and habitat differences. It illustrates how differential survival can change population composition when camouflage conditions change. Its outputs do not reproduce a historical time series without calibration, and a background-color rule cannot substitute for field evidence about resting behavior, predator choice, or the genetic mechanism that produces the actual moth phenotypes.
