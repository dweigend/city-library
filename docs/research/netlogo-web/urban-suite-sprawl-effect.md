# Urban Suite - Sprawl Effect

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 127 words

The scientific question is whether local attraction and declining site quality can generate dispersed development. In NetLogo's Urban Suite - Sprawl Effect model, seekers move toward attractive patches, settle probabilistically and reinforce activity, while overused sites undergo an abrupt attractiveness reset. The selected literature connects this mechanism to feedback-driven residential dispersion: the acknowledged SOME ancestry supplies an empirical modeling challenge [1]; historical urban-growth simulation offers a calibrated spatial comparator [2]; urban-expansion forecasts connect development patterns to environmental consequences [3]. The source acknowledges the reset as unrealistic; no zoning, market-clearing prices or infrastructure constraints are represented, so leapfrogging is a mechanism illustration. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.

For the model itself: * Felsen, M. and Wilensky, U. (2007). NetLogo Urban Suite - Sprawl Effect model. http://ccl.northwestern.edu/netlogo/models/UrbanSuite-SprawlEffect. Center for Connected Learning and Computer-Based Modeling, Northwestern University, Evanston, IL.

Scientific origin recorded in Info: This model was based on a model originally written by William Rand and Derek Robinson as part of the Sluce Project at the University of Michigan. For more about the original model (SOME) that was the basis for this model, please see: Brown D.G., Robinson D.T., Nassauer J.I., An L., Page S.E., Low B., Rand W., Zellner M., and R. Riolo (In Press) "Exurbia from the Bottom-Up: Agent-Based Modeling and Empirical Requirements." Geoforum. This model was then extended and expanded during the Sprawl/Swarm Class at Illinois Institute of Technology in Fall 2006 under the supervision of Sarah Dunn and Martin Felsen, by the following group of students: Christian Eichinger, Sara Martinez-Bravo, Kit Ottsen, John Wolters. See http://www.sprawlcity.us/ for more details. Further modifications and refinements were made by members of the Center for Connected Learning and Computer-Based Modeling before releasing it as an Urban Suite model. The Urban Suite models were developed as part of the Procedural Modeling of Cities project, under the sponsorship of NSF ITR award 0326542, Electronic Arts & Maxis. Please see the project web site ([http://ccl.northwestern.edu/cities/](http://ccl.northwestern.edu/cities/)) for more information.
