# GasLab Single Collision

Research status: complete. Literature research only; implementation acceptance is separate.

NetLogo GasLab Single Collision investigates how one elastic encounter redistributes momentum and kinetic energy. Two initialized particles collide along a selected line of centers; velocity components are transformed through the center-of-mass frame and displayed before and after the encounter. Establishes elastic molecular collisions as the elementary kinetic mechanism [1]. Demonstrates the computational importance of resolving hard-particle collisions [2]. Provides a contrasting continuous-force molecular-dynamics method, making numerical representation choices explicit [3]. The source isolates a planar pair event rather than many-body equilibration; molecular deformation, internal degrees of freedom and dissipative collision physics are absent. The simulation illustrates why individual particle speeds can change while the pair conserves total momentum and kinetic energy. Reproducing these patterns establishes rule fidelity, not empirical validation.
