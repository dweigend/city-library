# CRISPR Bacterium

Status: complete. Scientific research only.

How can a bacterium acquire sequence-specific memory of viral exposure? NetLogo CRISPR Bacterium follows a single cell confronted by viruses and represents recognition through stored sequence information. Original resistance experiments [1], small-RNA defense research [2], and work on viral-DNA-driven acquisition [3] supply direct scientific context. The served model distinguishes adaptive from innate protection while compressing molecular machinery into visible events. It omits many biochemical constraints, diverse CRISPR–Cas architectures, and the environmental variation surrounding real cells. Survival after previous exposure illustrates the logic of acquired specificity under the chosen rules. It does not establish a measured immune efficiency, reproduce the complete molecular pathway, or imply that all bacteria possess the same defense system.
