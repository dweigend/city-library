# Segregation

Status: **complete** · Checked: 2026-11-05 · Mini-text: 122 words

The scientific question is whether mild local similarity preferences can produce strong residential separation. In NetLogo's Segregation model, two agent types relocate when their neighboring same-type share falls below a specified satisfaction threshold. The selected literature connects this mechanism to micro-to-macro residential sorting: local preferences generate aggregate segregation without central coordination [1]; economic extensions test how additional incentives change segregation dynamics [2]; social context limits interpretation of physically similar spatial patterns [3]. Binary types and unrestricted relocation omit discrimination, housing costs and institutional constraints; local satisfaction is a stipulated rule rather than a measured universal preference. The original NetLogo behavior is the baseline for reimplementation. This simulation offers a controlled thought experiment, and agreement with its output does not establish empirical validation.
